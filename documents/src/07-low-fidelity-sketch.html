1. What these are and are not

Boxes and grey bars, deliberately. These sketches fix what goes where and nothing else — no colours, no type, no spacing, no final wording. Where a grey bar appears, the content is real but its exact words are not decided.

Two things are worth noticing in all four. The month-to-date strip sits above the tab bar on every screen, because it is the one figure worth seeing while doing something else. And there is no top app bar: on a four-tab app with no navigation hierarchy it would cost a fifth of the screen to display a word the tab bar already shows.

  1. Day heading with its own total. Today and yesterday are named rather than dated. One announcement to a screen reader: "Today, R150.00".
  2. One expense. Category mark, then the note — or the category name when there is no note — then the category, then the amount. The whole row is the tap target for editing.
  3. Swipe right-to-left to delete. One direction only, because there is no undo. The same action is reachable without the gesture for screen-reader users.
  4. Four tabs, with the month-to-date strip above them. Both are present on every screen.
  5. The amount, given the most room on the screen. Focused on open with the decimal keypad already up, so logging an expense needs no preparatory tap. The hint beneath it explains that a comma and a full stop both work.
  6. Date and note, deliberately small. Both are usually left alone: the date defaults to today and most expenses need no note.
  7. Sixteen categories, two to a row. Two rather than four so each chip has room for its label beside its glyph — the grid must be readable without relying on colour.
  8. One primary action, disabled until the amount is a number greater than zero.
  1. Month stepper. Back is always available; forward is greyed at the current month, because there is nothing ahead of it.
  2. The total, then the daily rate underneath. The rate divides by the days elapsed, so mid-month it reads as a rate rather than as an artificially low figure.
  3. Donut or bars, remembered between launches.
  4. At most six slices. The smallest are merged into "+N more" with the total preserved. The legend names every slice with its share and amount, so two similar colours are never the only way to tell them apart.
  5. A card tile shows the shop and the last four digits — never the whole number on a list that might be read over a shoulder.
  6. Adding a card is a row at the end of the list rather than a floating button, which keeps the gesture the same as everywhere else in the app.
  7. Tapping a card fills the screen with the code and raises brightness, because till scanners struggle with a dim screen. Below it, the number in text, for when the scanner gives up and the cashier types it.

2. What these sketches decided

QuestionAnswer, and why
Where does the month total live?Above the tab bar, on every screen. It is the figure the app exists to keep in front of you, and it belongs to no single screen.
Is there a top app bar?No. Four tabs, no hierarchy, nothing to title — it would cost a fifth of the screen to repeat a word the tab bar already shows.
Is Add a tab or a floating button?A tab, as on iOS. It is the most frequent action and it deserves a fixed, predictable place rather than a button that floats over content.
How wide are the category chips?Two to a row, so every chip fits its label beside its glyph. Four to a row would have forced icon-only chips and made the grid unreadable to anyone who does not know the icons.
How is deleting done?A swipe in one direction, plus an equivalent action that needs no gesture. One direction because there is no undo.
Where does editing happen?In the same form as adding, opened over the tabs. One screen, one set of rules.
Honest sequencing

These sketches were drawn after the screens were built, from the layouts as implemented — the project went straight from an existing iOS app to Compose without wireframing first. That is recorded as a gap in the lifecycle documentation rather than disguised here: the risk of skipping this step is a translated iOS layout instead of an Android one, and the decisions table above is where that was checked after the fact.
